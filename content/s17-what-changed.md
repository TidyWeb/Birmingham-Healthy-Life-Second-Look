## Re-testing the yellows out loud {#re-testing-the-yellows-out-loud}

Before I tick investigated and recorded, I must be able to say what could go wrong, who it would happen to, what deals with it, and who is responsible. If I cannot say that in a sentence or two, the lamp stays orange.

### Q1. Shapes a decision that matters to someone's life?

"Nobody's decision is made by this work, but a named map could steer how others judge a place and the people in it. I state that it's descriptive, list what it must not be used for, show how uncertain the rankings are, and anyone can question a result through the Question this work form. I answer for it."

**Verdict: Passes.**

### Q2. Could a simpler non-AI process do it?

"Most of the findings come from plain statistics: the joined table, correlations and the neighbour-gap test. The two models answer one question those can't: how much seven measures explain together, in councils the model hasn't seen. I used the simplest models that do that, so every result can be explained in years. If someone is misled, it's by my explanation, and I answer for it."

**Verdict: Passes once fixed.** I have now made the stated fix on [Stage 5: Machine learning](stage-5-machine-learning.html), so I record this concern as investigated and recorded.

### Q3. Purpose vague, or likely to grow?

"The purpose is to describe how healthy life expectancy varies across Birmingham's areas, and S13 lists five things it must not be used for. If I reuse the work for anything new, I run Signal Box again first. The data and site are public, so I can't stop others reusing them; I can only make the limits plain."

**Verdict: Passes once fixed.** I have now made the stated fix on [Law and governance](law-and-governance.html#excluded-uses), so I record this concern as investigated and recorded.

### Q4. Benefit to me, cost to others?

"I gain assessment and portfolio evidence. People in the areas ranked lowest could bear stigma. I reduce that by showing uncertainty, describing areas not people, and refusing claims the evidence can't carry. Making a real inequality visible is also a benefit to them. But nobody from those areas has had a say in the work."

**Verdict: Re-judge after user testing.** Q4 stays orange until user testing with three Birmingham readers is recorded; I will then re-judge it. They are three Birmingham readers, not representatives of the affected communities.

**Update, 20 October 2026: open by decision.** I've decided Q4 stays orange and I go ahead anyway; [Why three lamps stay orange](s2-the-method.html#sbx) on The method gives my reasons.

### Q7. Sensitive data, or a stand-in for it?

"The model doesn't use ethnicity or deprivation, but its measures overlap with them: up to R² 0.743 for White British share and 0.898 for deprivation. Someone could read the model's results as claims about ethnic groups or deprived communities. I publish the overlap, use the model only to describe areas, and list health claims about ethnic groups as an excluded use. I kept the features because they're the measures the project studies. I answer for that choice."

**Verdict: Passes once fixed.** I have now made the stated fix on [Proxies, fairness and crime](proxies-fairness-and-crime.html#proxy-overlap), so I record this concern as investigated and recorded.

### Q8. Re-identification by combining fields?

"Small groups in a published table could point to individuals. I publish only MSOA summaries, areas of 5,000 to 15,000 people, and six broad ethnic groups instead of 19. No person-level records are published. I'd re-check if I moved to finer geography or smaller groups."

**Verdict: Passes.**

### Q10. Groups missing or thin?

"An MSOA average can hide smaller areas inside it whose experience is different, so people there are misdescribed. Notebook 09 measured it: 30.3% of fuel-poverty variation is within MSOAs. The map marks the 255 mixed areas, and I make no claims below MSOA level. I answer for it."

**Verdict: Passes.**

### Q11. Learning from past human decisions?

"Recorded crime reflects police recording as well as crime itself, so it can carry policing patterns onto named areas. I keep it because it's one of the measures this project studies. I tested the model without it: the results barely change (RMSE 1.645 with it, 1.655 without), so no conclusion depends on it. I excluded six city centres where crime figures reflect visitors rather than residents, and I label it as recorded crime, not crime. I answer for keeping it."

**Verdict: Passes once fixed.** I have now made the stated fix on [Proxies, fairness and crime](proxies-fairness-and-crime.html#crime-sensitivity), so I record this concern as investigated and recorded.

### Q14. Output treated as proof, not a prompt?

"A single rank makes an area look more certainly 'worst' than the estimates allow, and residents of those areas carry the label. I ran 10,000 simulations: only 35 of the 66 lowest areas are confidently in the bottom tenth. I show rankings as ranges and confidence bands, not a firm order. I answer for how they're presented."

**Verdict: Passes.**

### Q15. Errors fall more on some groups?

"The model is about equally accurate everywhere, but its errors point different ways: it over-predicts healthy life in the most deprived third by 0.46 years and under-predicts the least deprived by 0.31. So model predictions understate the real gap, and the people in the most deprived areas would look better off than they are. I publish that finding, and the published HLE figures, not model predictions, describe each area. I answer for it."

**Verdict: Passes.**

### Q17. Can't understand, correct or challenge the result?

"If I've described an area wrongly, someone who knows it should be able to put it right. Anyone with a free GitHub account can open a question on a page or result; I reply within 10 working days, check it against the source and notebooks, correct any error and log it with the date and reason. Needing a GitHub account is a limit of this route. I'm responsible."

**Verdict: Passes once fixed.** I have now made the stated fix on [Question this work](question-this-work.html), so I record this concern as investigated and recorded.

### Q20. Could failure cause psychological harm?

"Someone could find their own neighbourhood among the lowest and read it as a judgement on them, or people elsewhere could treat it as a label. Every map and ranking says it describes areas, not people, and associations, not causes, and shows how uncertain the ranking is. I answer for that wording."

**Verdict: Passes.**

### Q22. No tested way to pause, override or fall back?

"If a published result turns out wrong or harmful, people keep reading it until it's fixed. I can correct a page and rebuild the site; I've tested that route with a test question. Taking the whole site offline is written down but untested. Retirement and archiving are written into S14. I'm the one who decides."

**Verdict: Passes once fixed.** I have now made the stated fix on [Lifecycle](lifecycle.html#correction-route), so I record this concern as investigated and recorded.

### Q23. Could it be misused or used out of purpose?

"Area health, crime and ethnicity measures shown together could be lifted out to target, price or stigmatise places. I list five excluded uses, keep every finding descriptive, and mark associations as associations. The data are public, so I can't prevent misuse, only refuse it in my own use and make the limits impossible to miss."

**Verdict: Passes once fixed.** I have now made the stated fix on [Law and governance](law-and-governance.html#excluded-uses), so I record this concern as investigated and recorded.

### Q25. Depends on a supplier's model or data I can't inspect?

"Four inputs are other organisations' modelled estimates that I can't reproduce, and they may be revised. If they're wrong, my results are too. The source register marks each as modelled, with its limits and revision risk, and S14 says which notebooks to re-run when a source changes. I maintain the register."

**Verdict: Passes.**

Applying the sharper test moved Q4 back to orange. I record that as a finding of testing Signal Box on my own work.

## Still open {#still-open}

These three stay open by decision. I looked at each one and decided to go ahead anyway.

- **Q4 — Benefit to me, cost to others.** I gain assessment and portfolio evidence while people in named areas could bear stigma. I accept that as the cost of trying to get to the truth about these differences, and I keep it down with broad labels, honest uncertainty and a policy on naming places.
- **Q18 — Affected people left out of design and testing.** The study covers 660 areas and several million people. There's no practical way to involve them all, and publishing area-level statistics doesn't need their permission.
- **Q21 — Tested only in a demo.** This is a course project, built to be assessed, not for anyone to rely on, so it won't be tested in real use.

User testing added evidence (all green; see below), but it doesn't close these three. If a reader reports a new problem, I will record it and decide what to change.

## User testing {#user-testing}

Five people ran the QA test on the live site. I ran it first, on 18 October 2026, then on 20 October four testers I know personally, from a range of backgrounds including health, science and data. Every tester rated every task green: 24 out of 24. The test was built before the Plain view existed, so it covers the Detailed view (the full technical dashboard) and the story pages. The anonymised results are in the [assessment repository](https://github.com/TidyWeb/Code-Institute-DA-with-AI-Unit-3-Assessment/tree/main/data/testing). Before that, on 17 October 2026, I reviewed the dashboard and site myself. That review led to fixes for the dashboard search bug; the HLE, rank and band labels; the six-groups note in the active-measure panel; the visibility of the “Question this work” link; and the plain-English wording in S11. I record the results as part of the evaluation stage in [Lifecycle](https://tidyweb.github.io/Birmingham-Healthy-Life-Second-Look/story/lifecycle.html).
