Chapter S10

Stage 7: Dashboards and site

I made an area-level story, maps and dashboards. I need to be as clear about their limits as I am about their findings.

In short

I published named area results through a story site and dashboards. Signal Box found risks in certainty, purpose, challenge and evaluation; the answer is to state the limits and make the claims match the evidence.

What I did in Unit 2

I built a public story site, a Tableau dashboard and a Leaflet dashboard with area-level maps, values and comparisons. The project describes areas; it does not make an automated decision about a person.

What Signal Box asked

Q1 — Could the work shape a decision that matters to someone? Concern. No individual decision is made, but named maps are public and can inform how professionals and others compare places.

Q3 — Is the purpose vague or likely to grow? Concern. The project question is clear, but Unit 2 did not state excluded uses.

Q4 — Does the work mainly benefit me while others bear a cost? Concern. Signal Box recorded that I gain assessment and portfolio evidence while the project names places without involving local readers. The accuracy response is to show uncertainty and avoid claims beyond the data. I've since settled Q4 by executive decision; see Why three lamps stay orange on The method.

Q14 — Could an output be treated as proof rather than a prompt? Concern. Exact values and ranks can appear firmer than the estimate intervals allow.

Q17 — Can someone understand, question or challenge the description? Concern. Unit 2 had no public route to question how an area was described.

Q18 — Were affected people left out of design and testing? Concern. No residents, community groups or local professionals tested the dashboards.

Q20 — Could a failure lead to harm? Concern. Signal Box found no physical risk. Its accuracy concern is that a map could overstate what area estimates show.

Q21 — Was the work tested only by its creator? Concern. I checked the dashboards myself, not with real users.

Q22 — Is there a tested way to pause, override or fall back? Concern. The static site can be taken down, but Unit 2 had no written correction, takedown or archive procedure.

Q23 — Could the work be used outside its purpose? Concern. Public combinations of health, crime, ethnicity and food-environment measures need explicit purpose limits and caveats so association is not presented as explanation.

Questions with no concern raised. Q12: no consequential decisions are automated. Q13: there is no reviewer workflow. Q16: AI use is disclosed. Q19: the project does not change jobs or monitoring. Q24: I am named as owner, although a review cadence is not recorded. Q26: the UK project is not deployed in the EU or in a listed high-risk area. Q27: the work is for Birmingham and should not be generalised to other places. None of them raised a concern, but that isn't a claim that every part of the project is safe.

What I found

Signal Box found a clear gap between publishing a dashboard and having evidence that people can interpret, question or correct it. I have not treated a creator-only check as user testing.

Two views, two audiences

Unit 2's dashboard was built for someone like me: someone happy to pick a measure from a list, read a rank range and interpret a colour scale. Signal Box's questions about understanding and challenge (Q14 and Q17) made me ask who else would use it. My tutor Vasi put it simply: a senior manager with no data background should be able to ask the dashboard a plain question and get a plain answer.

Given that the dashboard had to work for technical and non-technical audiences alike, I decided to build a Plain view. It carries a set of key questions, already chosen, so a non-technical reader can draw the main points out of the project without knowing how to work the map.

Having built it, I set up a guided test preview. A special link switches on a panel that takes the reader through six short tasks, one at a time, and asks after each whether it was clear, with a traffic-light rating. I sent the link by WhatsApp to four people I know personally, professionals from health, science and data, ranging from non-technical to highly technical, so they could run it on a work laptop in a few minutes. Each began in the Plain view. The tasks then took them into the Detailed view and the story pages, where the harder ideas are: rank ranges, the chance of being in the lowest tenth, and why ethnicity is shown in broad groups.

All four rated every task green: 24 out of 24. That included the reader who described themselves as non-technical, so even the detailed material was clear to someone without a data background.

So the dashboard now has two views. It opens in the Plain view: plain questions answered in plain words, with broad labels such as "clearly among the lowest" instead of exact ranks. The Detailed view, the full technical dashboard, is one click away for readers who want the measures, the rank ranges and the model.

What changes

The site now states the use limits and carries uncertainty and association caveats. The public question route is on Question this work. User testing was done on 20 October 2026, and every task was rated green (What changed). Corrections and retirement are in Lifecycle.
